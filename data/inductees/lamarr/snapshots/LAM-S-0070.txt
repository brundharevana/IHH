FACTORIES BACK
WAR BONDS PLAN
Ten Percent Savings Idea
Given Impetus by Movie
Stars Personal Pleas
One phase of the motion picture
industry’s war bond drive has al
ready proved itself a sweeping suc
cess—a phase that will have no
specific bearing on the industry’s
billion-dollar sales objective for
September. This is the vastly in
creased number of factories and
war production plants that have
stepped into line on the ten per
cent pay savings plan since the
drive started.
Under the impetus of visits from
Hollywood stars, scores of large fac
tories have already reported striking
increases in their payroll deduction
enrollments. In every instance
where a "star rally” has been held
at a factory, the workers have in
creased their previous pay-envelope
war bond deductions to bring the
present average well above ten per
cent.
The Treasury Department has ex
pressed itself as vitally concerned
with the payroll plan, stating that
if deductions can be brought up to
ten per cent of payrolls in all types
of industries throughout the coun
try, the present voluntary system
of bond selling can be continued.
Should the movie industry’s Sep
tember efforts raise the August pay
deduction average of 5.3 per cent
substantially closer to the 10 per
cent goal, the drive would be con
sidered a success, Washington
states, even if the billion dollar gen
eral sales objective is not attained.
For this reason the film stars who
are now covering three hundred
cities throughout the country are
devoting approximately half their
time to factory rallies, the plants
being designated by the war savings
staff in each locality.
The actual black and white audit
ing results of the factory rallies will
not begin showing up until October,
but from that point on will have
provided a permanent material con
tribution to the work of the Treas
ury Department and war savings
staff. In the factories visited to
date including a very large quota
covered on Labor Day, male stars
and glamor girls seem to have had
almost uniformly successful results.
After Hedy Lamarr's visits to the
Johnson and Johnson plant in
Elizabeth, N. J., one hundred per
cent enrollment for the bond sav
ings plan was announced. At the
York Safe company, in York, Pa.,
Miss Lamarr raised $20,000 in addi
tional salary pledges during a brief
visit.
Jimmy Cagney’s vehement and
straight-from-the-shoulder talks to
thousands of workers in Gary, Ham
mond and East Chicago steel mills
brought an earlier average of four
per cent savings enrollment up
ahead of the ten per cent mark. In
Michigan City, 12,000 Studebekare
and Bendix workers signed 12,000
pledges after Cagney’s visit.
Walter Pidgeon, Joan Leslie and
Adolphe Menjou produced equally
heartening results at the giant
Kaiser shipyards in Salem, Oregon.
Charles Laughton, Ann Rutherford
and Virginia Gilmore promoted an
enormous increase of ten per cent
salary allocations in the New Ha
ven, Hartford and New Britain
areas.
Immediate results, on an unan
ticipated scale, were reported after
Edward Arnold and Prances Dee
had addressed 1,000 personnel di
rectors at a luncheon at the Pea
body luotel in Memphis. After the
visits of the same stars to East St.
Louis, 111., salary pledges jumped
from 8.2 per cent to 12.4 per cent, a
45 per cent increase.
Another striking development of
the drive, this time through the ef
forts of Hedy Lamarr, was reported
in Newark, N. J. Col. Franklin
D'OUer, president of the Prudential
Insurance company, pledged that
Prudential's 20,000 salesmen would
be given quotas and act as “Cogi
mandos" in the bond drive.

BROOKLYN NEWS
Plans for promoting the sale of
defense bonds and stamps will be
made at a meeting of the Lithuan
ian Council for the Defense of
America tonight at 8 o’clock at
Venta hall, 103 Green street. Rep
resentatives of the council recently
participated in the sale at Liberty
House on the Green and another
session is expected to be arranged.
Jacob Treciokas, president of the
council, which includes delegates
from 16 independent Lithuanian or
ganizations, has announced that
Lithuanian residents of the city
have purchased $300,000 worth of
bonds already, as far as has been
called to the council’s attention,
and that the figure is expected to
reach the half-million mark shortly.
Various events to boost defense
activities will be planned tonight.
