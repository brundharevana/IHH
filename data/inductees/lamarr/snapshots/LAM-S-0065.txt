LABOR-CAPITAL
PEACE IS URGED
WELLESLEY HILLS, Mass.,
Oct. 6 (I.N.S.).—Employers will
most satisfactorily further their
relations with trade unions if they
cease to think in terms of legal
restraints which they may exer
cise against them, Edwin S.
Smith, member of the National
Labor Relations Board, told the
twenty fourth annual National
Business Conference at Babson’s
Institute today.
He said successful dealing with
trade unions depends on the
growth of mutual confidence be
tween the employer and his work
ers’ representatives.
Smith said it had been com
monly asserted that the board’s
partiality toward labor is shown
by the fact that in only a com
paratively few decisions does the
board find in the employer’s
favor.
“The statment is true,” he
said, “but the inference of par
tiality drawn from it is com
pletely misleading. It overlooks
the fact that each case decided
by the board has passed through
a highly selective sifting process
before it is brought to our atten
tion in final form for adjudica
tion.”

Congress, a group of 30 Demo
cratic Congressmen have agreed
upon the basic form of a new wage
hour bill as a substitute for the
Administration's Black-Connery
bill.
The new bloc, it was disclosed to
day, regards enactment of national
minimum wages and maximum
hours legislation in the next ses
sion of Congress, either special or
regular as inevitable.
May Split Party
The substitute plan, which may
split Democratci ranks in the
House, is based upon expansion of
power and authority of the Fed
eral Trade Commission.
The plan is:
1. Amendment of section 5 of
anti-trust act, to permit Federal
Trade Commission to declare as
an unfair trade practice pay
ment of subnormal wages, exces
sive hours or use of child labor.
2. Upon declaration by com
mission affecting an industry,
shipment of goods into a State
having labor standards above
that of the industry held to be
guilty of unfair trade practice
is prohibited.
Representative Arthur Lamneck
(D.) of Ohio will introduce the
new bill on the first day of the
new session.
During the six weeks since Con
gress adjourned, the Democratic
group, chiefly through exchang
ing ideas by correspondence, has
united on the new substitute plan.
FIGHT IN HOUSE
Since the Black-Connery wage
hours bill passed the Senate, the
battle in the new session will cen
ter in the House.
“It is our belief that 90 per
cent of Southern Democrats and
a large group from other sec
tions, particularly the middle
and far west, will support the
substitute program,” said Lam
neck.
“Enactment of legislation Is
inevitable. The Black-Connery
bill is regarded by excellent
lawyers as unconstiutional. But
it also would have a bad effect
ou the country.
“The new bill does not fix
wages and hours. It will strike
i at the ‘sweat shop,’ encourage
action by State legislatures to
aid the group for which aid is
sought, will not interfere with
collective bargaining and is
flexible.”
Expert legal advisers, Lamneck
said, are putting the finishing
touches on the bill. Copies will be
sent to all of the 435 members of
the House and an active drive will
be opened to win support.
