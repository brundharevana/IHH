family- activities.
Although most of the new and
older residents have adjusted
themselves to each other and get

ing between all parties concerned,

ter which would explain to the
people of the town just what
some of the new residents be
lieved and respected.
The Mayberry Village residents
have spoken honestly in their let
ter although, of course, they may
be mistaken in some of their
conclusions. The Gazette re
quests all its readers to weigh
carefully all that is said in the
letter and it also requests that
no controversy be continued and
that everybody work together for
the good of the town and the war

about everything has been said
openly or whispered on all sides
of the problem. The Gazette be
lieves that both old and new resi
dents love East Hartford and are
fine examples of the American
way of life. The letter, signed by
Charles T. Hanley, chairman of
a special committee of the May
berry Village Association, reads
as follows:—
Dear Sir:—
Referring to an article written
by a Mr. James Gibbs in your
August 28th issue,
We, the people of Mayberry Vil
lage, wish to place ourselves on
record as being heartily in agree
ment with Mr. Gibbs when he
mentions the need for sidewalks,
playgrounds, etc., in various
places throughout East Hartford.
In fact, he might have gone a

lack of fire “call boxes” in the

been laid out for some time and

AIRCRAFT CLOSES
FOR WEEKEND
The Pratt and Whitney Divis
ion of United Aircraft will close
at midnight tonight for a three
days holiday for almost all em
ployees. It will open again at
midnight Monday. The Hamilton
Standard Division will not close.
Labor Day
Labor Day 1942 finds us all
workers —workers and fight
ers in a war against tyranny,
against despots who would
make us all slaves. We must
meet their attack as a united
nation —not as a loose con
federation of groups or class
es. Unfortunately we have
not yet fully grasped the
fact that this is a War of
Survival of all the people,
not a People's War in the
proletarian sense. There is
too much lobbying and prat
ing of classes and groups
when what we really need is
not a consciousness of class
but an awareness of unity.
This is not a war for any
one group of citizens. It is a
war in which all Americans
have a part. We cannot fight
Hitler by crushing any group
In this country. We cannot
destroy the labor movement
and retain free business en
terprise. We cannot destroy
Industry and keep a free la
bor movement or avoid col
lective farming. We cannot
damn one minority without
damning all.
In the totalitarian states it
was not Organized Labor
that suffered. Free associa
tions of employers were also
abolished. Neither collective
bargaining nor collaboration
of labor and industry was al
lowed.
We must remember these
things today for the enemy
is attacking us not merely
on land, on sea and in the
air, but also in our hearts
and heads, taking advantage
of every ignorance, of every
prejudice, of every weak
ness —setting group against
group, class against class.
We must not be blind to
those things for which we
are fighting—freedom of
thought and discussion and
worship. We must not mere
ly concede them —we must
practice them. Intolerance
taps the moral strength of
its adherents. Those who
regiment are slaves no less
than those who are regiment
ed. What we must do is to
work In fellowship, in the
spirit of friendliness.
There should be a little
more of the Golden Buie and
a little less Blackstone in our
relations with each other.
Our entire system of life is
on trial. How it proves itself
will determine the future.
After all —we are all work
ers no matter what our job
—and any man with an in
surance policy or money in
the bank is a partner in In
dustry. When we have had
prosperity—all of us had it;
when depression struck —all
felt it.
We must learn how to
work together: how —the
way of cooperation; work
coordinated actions for the
nation as a whole; together
—with mutual trust and re
spect. This applies to every
one: those who operate our
factories or who work in
them; those who toil on
farms, In offices or at home.
All must work together with
an awareness that what they
do or neglect doing will de
termine the future of the
country.
We have no right to take
“class’ sides. We must work
for the common gpod, in
groups if we choose, but we
must never forget that the
welfare of the nation as a
whole transcends the private
Interest of any group. This
is democracy.
East Hartford, Conn.

New York City Lady
Wins Limerick Prize
As New System Tried
Almost Everyone
Violates Rules So
Judges Hopelessly
Stare At Entries
Helen R. Kirk of 884 Riverside
Drive, New York City, wins this
week’s limerick prize of two dol
lars in war stamps in the Ga
zette’s weekly contest in which a
prize is given each week. We
think, too, that Miss or Mrs.
Kirk has possibly violated the
rules of good form in limericks
and the judges will probably nev
er hear the end of an argument
on their decision.
Since The Gazette hasn’t the
slightest idea who the winner is,
we would appreciate it if she
would write us and inform us
about herself and also tell us
how she happened to hear about
the limerick contest.
Strangely, more than one hun
dred entries had more than eight
syllables in the last line of the
limerick when only eight were
proper if it were to rfiatch the
first line. The judges were about
to discard more than one hun
dred entries and then decided
that some of them “sounded” all
right even though an extra syl
lable was inserted. The winner,
for instance, used the word
“economy” in her winning line.
The judges felt that this had the
effect of 3 syllables when read
with the proper tone of voice.
Try it and see for yourself be
fore you holler.
Another. reason why the judg
es chose Helen R. Kirk’s entry
was because many readers sub
mitted exactly the same line and
the judges felt that if the limer
ick contestants themselves
thought it was good, it must be.
The Kirk entry had the earliest
postmark so the prize was
awarded to her.
It should be pointed out that
even members of the Contest
Club of the Aif, who usually fol
low rules, were among the most
frequent violators of rules in
last week’s contest. For instance,
Mrs. Muriel B. Cressy of 199
Ellington Road, submitted a line

WHEEL PARADE
AND EGG RACE
ON LABOR DAY
Tennis Matches And
Swimming Events
Also Planned
East Hartford will celebrate
Labor Day with a series of activ
ities for the public in the Burn
side Playground. The events will
start at 10 a. m. and continue in
to the evening.
At 10 a. m., the competition
will start to determine the men’s
doubles championship in tennis.
All pairs should register at the
Burnside Pool at sometime be
fore the start of the matches.
Field events will also start at
10 a. m.
The wheel parade is scheduled
for 2:30 p. m. Anything on
wheels, bicycles, tricycles, carts,
etc., are eligible for participa
tion. A prize will be awarded for
the best decorated entry.
Running events will start im
mediately after the wheel parade
and then will come a novelty egg
and spoon race and a greased
watermelon race. Swimming
events will begin at 4:30 p. m.
and will include free style and
relay races and diving competi
tions.
There will be an exhibition of
formation swimming shortly be
fore 6 p. m. under the direction
of Miss Shirley Griffen. Judges
for the water event are Mitchell
Holmgren, George Remmert and
Shirley Griffen.

EASTWOOD SELLS
SIX HUNDRED IN
VICTORY BONDS
American Legion,
War Mothers, Army
And Navy, Boy
Scout Nights
Are Planned
More than six hundred dollars
worth of War bonds were sold at
the Eastwood Theatre during the
first two days this week of its
Salute to Heroes program to
stimulate the sale of bonds.
Manager Frank Weinstein of the
theater expressed the opinion
that East Hartford is doing its
share in the nation-wide drive in
theaters to raise one billion dol
lars for the war.
Mr. Weinstein announced that
there will be nights set aside for
the American Legion, the Army,
Navy and Marines; Boy Scouts,
War Mothers and various organi
zations before the end of the
month with special ceremonies
on each night. The local cam
paign opened Tuesday with a
street parade, talks by Father
Glynn, Mayor Paul Britt and
Wesley C. Carroll. More than a
thousand persons were in front
of the theater for the initial ral
ly-
Mr. Weinstein asserted that af
ter all these years, the movie in
dustry' has finally gotten itself
into something that really merits
that much-used word “colossal.”
Nothing short of that adjective
can begin to describe the all-out
industry effort being put into the
September “Salute to Our He
roes” war bond and stamp drive,
just getting under way from
coast to coast, from Radio City
Music Hall in New York to the
smallest, one-night-a-week movie
house in Alaska.
The War Activities Committee
of the motion picture industry is
fully aware that it has undertak
en to do a “miracle” war job.
They have set a billion-dollar
quota for September—a mark
hit only once and that in Janu
ary, following Pearl Harbor. The
Treasury War Savings Staff it
self rather doubts that a billion
dollar target can be hit in Sep
tember and probably will set a
lower official quota. However,
the movie people still are shoot
ing at the billion and as spear
head of the month’s drive hope
to spark the sales to the fantas
tic total. This will mean, they
warn, that local committees prob
ably will have to exceed their
official Treasury quota by 25 per
cent.
In Hollywood, September will
be a strange month. Some fifty
of the outstanding stars will be
roaming the country instead of
making movies. They have al
ready started their “Stars Over
America” bond-selling tours,
which will take various individ
ual stars and groups of them in
to some 300 large and small cit
ies for as many bond-rallies,
aimed at a total $300,000,000 sale!
In Washington, the drive had
its world-premiere August 31,
with Secretary Morgenthau send
ing the stars on their way fol
lowing an official luncheon and
bond-rally on the Treasury steps.
In the group were Hedy Lamarr,
Irene Dunne, Ann Rutherford,
Greer Garson, Edward Arnold,
Virginia Gilmore, James Cagney,
Charles Laughton, Bing Crosby,
Bob Hope and others.
But the “Buy a Bond To Honor
Every Mother’s Son in Service”
drive is not a ballyhoo cam
paign. Every item on the pro
gram aims at actual bond-selling.
Some 2000 factories will be visit
ed by the stars to stimulate the
payroll deduction plan. Every
luncheon and meeting will be a
success only if bonds are sold in
huge amounts.

East Hartford residents will
hold a massive town-wide rally
for victory on Saturday, October
3, at the Burnside Playground to
promote the sale of war bonds
and stamps and to stimulate civil
defense.
Tentative plans call for field
events, baseball games, a tug of
war across the wading pool and
a massive demonstration of ci
vilian defense work in the even
ing. Other features of the event
will be announced from time to
time.
It will be the first rally of its
kind ever held in East Hartford.
Already, other towns are plann
ing similar events. State Defense
officials like the idea so much
that they will recommend that
all towns in the State hold such
patriotic demonstrations at some
time this fall .The idea was first
suggested by Ralp Neal and was
taken up by a large number of
members of the general public.
At the request of members of
the public, The Gazette will spon
sor the event.
President Paul E. Britt of the
Town Council will be honorary
chairman of the rally and Ralph
Neal will be general chairman.
Charles Paddock will be general
chairman of the civilian defense
activities. Publisher Edward B.
Stevens of The Gazette, Mr.
Britt, Mr. Neal, and Mr. Pad
dock will operate as an advisory
committee. State officials have
already given full backing to’the
plan.
The event will be for every
man, woman and child in East
Hartford. Euthusiasm for the ral
ly has already been manifested
by many residents in the new de
velopments such as Mayberry
Village and Milbrook Park and
it is expected that large delega
tions from these projects will
participate.
Tentative plans also call for
community singing and dancing.
