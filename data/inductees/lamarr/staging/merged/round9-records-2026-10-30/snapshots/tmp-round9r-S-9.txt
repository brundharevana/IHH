Colorado and Wyoming
Are Hit by Snowstorm
By the Associated Press
DENVER, April 11.—Colorado
and Southern Wyoming were in
the grip of a snowstorm today.
Airlines and bus schedules were
disrupted by the spring storm
which also was blamed for one
Colorado traffic death and nu
merous other accidents.
The snow had piled up 10
inches deep at Fort Bridger, Wyo.
The heaviest falls reported in
Colorado were 7 inches at Akron
in the arid eastern portion of the
State, 5 inches at Denver and 3
at Colorado Springs.
The Weather Bureau said the
storm was slowly spreading
southward into New Mexico and
eastward into Kansas and the
southwest corner of Nebraska.

Production and Job
Outlook Held Good
By Congress Experts
By J. A. O'Leary
Congressional experts reported
today that the outlook for high
levels of production and em
ployment remains good, despite
the bearish attitude of the stock
market toward a Korean truce.
The staff of the Joint House-
Senate Economic Committee
pointed out that Korean war
expenditures account for only
10 per cent of overall military
spending, and ttteir reduction
following an armistice would not
warrant “a change in business
or consumer expectations, or any
significant cuts in private spend
ing.”
The experts warned, however,
that;
“The only real danger is that
an unjustified psychological re
action will set in similar to that
of July, 1950, but in the reverse
direction, resulting in wide
spread retrenchment in anticipa
tion of reductions in Govern-
demand larger than ac
tually developed.”
In submitting their report to
Senator Flanders, Republican, of
Vermont, vice chairman of the
committee, the staff urged the
public to remember that a
“truce” is not a “settlement”
and will not bring with it auto
matically marked changes in
Federal programs or alter
the sustaining economic forces
ahead.
The experts suggested that
private investment plans should
not be altered by a Korean truce
from the high levels previously
in prospect. They said present
investment plans appear to be
based on long-run considerations
which are reasonably secure
from fluctuations due to a
Korean truce.
The experts gave three rea
sons why present investment
plans should not be materially
affected by a Korean truce,
namely:
1. They were made in expecta
tion of a cut in defense spending
after this year’s peak.
2. There is no evidence of ex
cess capacity in industries where
additional investment is planned.
3. The demand for residential
construction, continued high
birth rates and high levels of
consumer incomes and savings
sufficient to turn housing needs
into effective demand.
