WHOSE FOOT SLIPPED?
AN EDITORIAL
“As to the special emergency grant based on hur
ricane conditions, the department (state highway) has
received no official notification, and thus has planned no
projects under it. This hurricane relief program will not
get any attention this meeting (of the state highway com
mission) owing to lack of federal action in notifying the
state.”—Gibb Gilchrist, state highway engineer.
On Sept. 29 The Brownsville Herald carried a news
story saying that $4,000,000 was to be made available
for emergency road building work in the hurricane-strick
en counties of the Low-er Rio Grande Valley.
Again on Oct. 11 The Brownsville Herald carried an
Associated Press dispatch out of Washington saying that
the sum of $2,670,000 had been allotted for that pur
pose by the Public Works administration, which sum was
to be added to $6,000,000 to be supplied by the Relief
administration.
That $8,670,000 is available, or it is to be made
available, for emergency road building in the drouth ter
ritory of the Panhandle and the hurricane-swept Valley
has been common knowledge for a week.
Yet—because of no official notification from the
federal government the hands of the State Highway com
mission are tied, and the Valley counties suffer.
John Woods, chairman of the State Highw-ay com
mission, helped engineer the emergency appropriation.
Marvin Jones of Amarillo, congressman from the drouth
territory, helped get it through.
But—no official notification has been received and
we continue to have thousands on relief rolls, men are
clamoring for work, children are clamoring for food.
Cameron county has today 92 miles of road, we are
told on unquestionable authority, on which the grading
has been completed and the structures built. On much
of this mileage bids have already been advertised but
no contracts let because of failure to sell the last batch
of Cameron county road bonds.
The stage is set, ready to begin almost immediate
road work, and road work gives lots of employment.
But—no road work under this huge emergency grant
can be considered at the present meeting of the State
Highway commission, because, forsooth, the commission
has not been notified officially that the money has been
made available.
W’hose foot has slipped?
We do not know, but we do know that there should
be a man somewhere in Cameron or Hidalgo counties
w’ith enough influence in official Washington to get a
letter written to the State Highway commission notifying
the commission that this money is available.
The Public W’orks administration has announced
through the press that the money is available, for heaven’s
sake let’s get what is coming to us while it will do some
good to the thousands who need assistance and need it
NOW.
