PLAV FACILITIES
Park Employes Face 37 Days
Furlough in Addition
to Pay Cut.
District government budget estimates,
now pending in the House Appropria
tions Committee, reduce the items for
sports for the coming fiscal year from
$38,000 to $20,000,' which will mean a
sharp curtailment of facilities for public
outdoor recreation, park officials said
yesterday, after a study of the figures.
The officials are unable to say, as yet.
just what sports might be affected.
Perhaps the most serious feature of the
reduction, officials asserted, is the fact
that because of depleted funds for pay
ing salaries, each employe of the Office
of Public Buildings and Public Parks
will have to take 37 days' administrative
furlough during the coming year, start
ing July 1. This will be in addition to
the projected 15 per cent pay slash,
they asserted.
The salary item has been cut in the
District estimates from $400,000 to
$300,000—a 25 per cent cut to make up
the 15 per cent individual shrinkage in
salaries. The additional 10 per cent
must be made up by placing employes
on administrative furlough, officials ex
plained.
Lieut. Col. U. S. Grant. 3d. director
of public buildings and public parks,
asserted that his office would take its
cut proportionately with the District
government. He inclined to the view
that inasmuch as the voters desired a
reduction in governmental expenditures,
the country must expect this.
All construction work in the parks
has been eliminated in the estimates.
There Ls a feeling among certain
officials, however, that the administra
tion proposes to insert construction
items in a general public works meas
ure. designed to stimulate employment.
The general expense figure was cut
from $500,000 to $333,000. the officials
said. For the first time, the United
States park police will be placed on an
administrative furlough, which will
work out at from 18 to 21 days per
officer. Salaries for the police are cut
from $180,000 to $145,000. Funds for
the equipment and uniform are trimmed
from $12,500 to $9,000.

A reduction of 47 in the number of
uniformed policemen will be necessary
if the 1934 budget is enacted in the!
form sent up to Congress by the Budget
Bureau, MaJ. Ernest W. Brown, super
intendent of police, said yesterday.
The budget cuts the appropriation
for the force $75,000 under its present
standard. Under the 15 per cent pay
cut, the salary of a policeman in his
first year of service is $1,615. Dividing
the salary into the amount of the re
duction yields the number of men to
be dropped.
Bean and lleadlcy on List.
There are several methods of accom
plishing the reduction, should legisla
tion make it necessary. The Commis
sioners now have the power to place
on the retired list any member of the
force who has passed his 60th birth
day, regardless of his physical condi
tion. A survey is now being made to
determine how many men in the de
partment are more than 60. The list,
when it is made up, will include two
inspectors. Thaddeus R. Bean and In
spector Albert J. Headley.
Aside from retiring those over 60,
the Commissioners can order retired
any who does not satisfy the Board of
Police Surgeons of his physical qualifi
cations for police duty. Maj. Brown
said he is planning a medical and
physical survey of every member of
the department next month to see
whether any of them should be re
tired for this reason. The examina
tion Is normally an annual affair, but
no examination was held in 1932.
Nine Vacancies Now.
Part of the cut can be accomplished
by the occurrence of vacancies through
deaths or resignations. There are now
nine vacancies In the basic strength
of 1,341 members, and thr^e addi
tional vacancies are expected to occur
in the near future.
Finally, if these methods are ex
hausted and still the force must be
cut down, the Commissioners could or
der members of the force laid off with
out pay. Not only is this last alterna
tive a possibility, but the Commission
ers would be compelled to do it rather
than Incur a deficiency in the salary
item, it was explained.
Maj. Brown said that whatever hap
pened. he would endeavor to keep the
same number of men on street duty by
relieving members of the force of other
assignments, as he considers his pres
ent street force an absolute minimum.
