ROW OVER BONNETS.
It was almost impossible to get a
good vieiv of the flood of 1903 On ac
count of the mist and fog which en
shrouded the flooded district. It was
only after the waters had receded con
siderably that a view of the Kaw val
ley could be obtained from the state
house, the top of the federal building;
or any other-point of observation. To
day, however, when the river her is
almost at Its maximum height the at
mospheric conditions are good and the
view unobstructed for miles up and
down the Kaw.
From the state house dome today
with the aid of field glasses one could
see distinctly up the river beyond
Rossvllle.
Probably the very best view of the
flooded Kaw valley In this vicinity is
from Martin's Hill on top of which is
Cedarcrest,- Frank ..P. MacLennan's
country home, .y..
Many sightseers visited the hill yes
terday and today and witnessed the
wonderful view of the flooded Kaw.
The aspect of the present flood is
entirely different from that of 1903
chiefly perhaps on account of the
weather. For with sunshine and a blue
sky it seems imposible for the valley
of the Kaw to be other than beautiful.
Even under the conditions now exist
ing the view from Martin's Hall is
grand, but five years ago, wrapped in
mist and gloom, it was sinister and
terrible.
The first comment of a spectator of
both floods was on this marked dif
ference.
The stench from the river five years
ago was sickening:- the stream was a
raging torrent filled with every sort of
debris and the drift wood was often
a precarious raft for living freight, the
domestic animals which .-had taken
refuge on it. Today from Martin's
Hill the Kaw was not unlike a great
peaceful lake, dotted with boat houses,
the farm buildings, which appeared to
be floating on its surface.
Under that smiling sky and with the
peaceful and beautiful view to the
south it is impossible to think of the
great expanse of water as a frightful
agent of destruction. The sight seer
expecting to be horrified is disap
pointed.
In width the river lacks something
of the Kaw in the first week of June
1903. Opposite Martin's Hill it is only
about two miles wide. The water is
well up around Menoken, the Upde
grafT farm is today like an embowered
-island, and all up and down the val
ley occasional fields of wheat and oats
are out of the water, although entire
ly surrounded. Beyond the northern
boundary of the swollen stream there
are many scattered ponds dotting the
farms at the foot of the bluffs. Five
years ago this land was entirely cov
ered, with only the buildings and the
highest trees emerging. The river
stretched from bluff to bluff.
Silver Lake is plainly seen with the
naked eye today. The river at this
point is broken out of its banks and
overflows the old river bed connecting
with the lake.
