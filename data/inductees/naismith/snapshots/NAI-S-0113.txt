BY FRANK GETTY.
United Prem Sports Editor.
NEW YORK—“Go invent a new
game that can be played by 18 men
indoors, at night, and in the win
ter,” were the instructions James
Naismith, young instructor of
physical education at the Spring
field Y. M. C. A. college of Spring
field, Mass., received in December,
1891.
Whereupon Naismitt>, now pro
fessor of physical education at the
University of Kansas, Lawrence,
invented the game of basketball.
Inventing a new indoor team
game back in 1891 was pioneering
work. Ice hockey was the only in
door game of importance, and in
door ice rinks were not common.
So it is not surprising that basket
ball enjoyed a phenomenal growth
almost from its outset.

Two peach baskets hung at op
posite ends of the room were the
goals. A soccer ball was used. As
there were 18 in the class, nine
men made up each team. The
court measured 35 feet by 45 feet,
about half the size of the present
standard court. The game was
governed by 13 rules worked out by
the inventor.
The essential features of the
game today were embodied in those
13 rules. A player could not run
with the ball. Personal contact
was illegal. A horizontal hoop was
the goal. Dr. Naismith rea
soned that all the men liked Rugby,
but that it was Impractical for in-,
door playing because it was too
rough. It was rough because of
the tackling and the tackling exist
ed because running with the ball
was allowed. Therefore, running
and tackling were eliminated.
v
The center tip, much debated this
season, but not yet abolished, also
was the original means of putting
the ball in play. Dr. Naismith is
a strong supporter of the center tip
today, believing that to abolish it
would be to remove one of the most
spectacular features of the game.
Any other method would slow up
the game, he says.
The principal changes in basket
ball today as compared with the
original peach basket game, accord
ing to the inventor, are those of
skill on the part of the players and
coaches. Early games were unpol
ished affairs, he recalls. The pres
ent day dribble is new, but its fore
runner, rolling the ball upon the
floor, was used in the original
game.
Dr. Naismith, who has been at
the University of Kansas since
1898, was born in Almonte, On
tario, Nov. 6, 1861. He received'! his
A.B. degree from McGill university
at Montreal in 1887 and later at
tended the Presbyterian Theologi
cal college at Montreal. In 1891 he
became instructor in physical edu
cation at Springfield. Two years
later he entered the Gross Medical
college at Denver, which, now is af
filiated with the University of Colo
rado, and received a degree of doc
tor of medicine. He came to the
University of Kansas in 1898 where
he has remained since.
Basketball still is the inventor’s
principal hobby. The discussion in
sporting circles throughout the
middle west this winter regarding
the abolition of the center tip has
aroused his Interest considerably.
“I’ve kept figures on the tip-off,”
said Dr. Naismith, “and I have
become assured that there is no ad
vantage, unfair, or otherwise, giv
en either to tall or short men by
its use.” Some had held the tip-off
gives an advantage to tall men over
short players.
“Fifty per cent of the times, the
other team gets the ball when its
opposing jumper tips the ball,” the
inventor said.

Fields, welterweight titleholder,
has a logical championship oppo
nent, Matchmaker Nate Lewis of
the Chicago Stadium today prom
ised that he will give the middle
west a 147-pound championship
fight just as soon as Fields Is will

Vancouver Irishman.
McLarnin provided himself the
outstanding welterweight contend
er when he beat Sammy Mandell,
the lightweight champion, in 10
rounds here Saturday night. Be
fore that fight Mandell and McLar
nin were considered the two men
who have a chance to beat Fields.

punching the lightweight champion.

FOX RIVER BALL
LOOP CUT TO
SIX TEAMS
APPLETON, Wis. Marinette
and Fond du Lac will not enter
teams in the Fox River Valley Base
ball league this year, it became
known today following a meeting
of league officials here.
A six-club federation, composed
of teams from Wisconsin Rapids,
Appleton, Neenah-Menasha, Kau
kauna, Green Bay and Kimberly-
Little Chute was formed at the pre
season meeting.
The circuit’s spring season will
open May 11 with Kimberly-Little
Chute at Green Bay; Neenah-Mena
sha at Appleton, and Wisconsin
Rapids at Kaukauna. Final games
will be played Sept. 7.
