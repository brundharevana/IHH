title: Press Copies of Letters Sent by the Chiefs
scopeAndContentNote: This series consists of letters sent by Chiefs of Division "L" to the Secretary of the Interior; members of Congress; officials of other government agencies, such as the Board on Geographic Names and the Lighthouse Board; state officials; chiefs of other General Land Office (GLO) divisions; attorneys; contractors; and other interested persons.  The letters concern requests for state maps and information needed for mapping purposes; the preparation and reproduction of maps and plats; and surveys of townships, national parks, states, and land districts.  Also included are reports and periodic statements of work, recommendations concerning the administration of the Division, requests for supplies and equipment, requests for samples from manufacturers in connection with bids, recommendations for awarding or withdrawing contracts, and reports of investigations of personnel efficiency and performance of assigned duties.

Frank Bond served as Division Chief from 1903 to 1907.  Included are several of his letters that concern maps to be exhibited at the Louisiana Purchase Exposition, the correct forms of place names, and a few that relate to his work on a commission to examine Yosemite Park lands.  There is also a 1906 letter that he wrote to the GLO Commissioner, after the Division was put in charge of matters relating to prehistoric sites.  In this letter, he recommended the resurvey and reassessment of land covered by a homestead entry in New Mexico that embraced Pueblo Bonito, the largest prehistoric ruin in the United States; and the resurvey and reassessment of three other nearby ruins.  In this letter, he likewise recommended an examination of the natural bridges area in southeastern Utah.  In his letter of December 28, 1906, he wrote to John Muir regarding the location of certain Petrified Forest remains in Arizona that were outside the boundaries of the Petrified Forest National Monument.
