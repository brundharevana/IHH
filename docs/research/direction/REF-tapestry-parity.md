## 0. One-paragraph answer

Tapestry's experience is a fixed grammar repeated per room: a narrated stop with captions and a slow camera move; when narration ends, three human voices and a "Dive Deeper" mode appear; Dive Deeper shows a numbered row of objects, and each click turns the camera to the object and fills a frosted right-hand panel with title, one image or video, a short note and a credit (UX §2, §11). None of that grammar depends on Gaussian splats. Every step maps onto plain HTML, `<audio>`/`<video>` with WebVTT, a native `<dialog>` or side panel, and a viewer that can turn to a stored view. Our closest equivalent of their "scene" is a constructed three.js room baked to a 4096x2048 panorama (spike S1: 414 KiB WebP, 896 KiB page in 3D mode, 84 KiB in Lite), with archival photos shown untouched as their own layer. The one thing a baked panorama cannot copy is the camera *moving through* the room (dolly and parallax); it can copy the turn-and-zoom to an object, which is what the visitor needs to see "this object" before reading about it. Recommendation in section C: baked panoramas for all scenes, a live three.js room only in the entrance hall, both built from the same room source.

## B. Patterns to copy closely, and where we deliberately differ

### B1. Copy closely
1. **The per-scene grammar**: narrate (30-60 s, captions on) -> reveal voices and objects -> object click turns the view and fills a side panel -> never auto-advance (items 12, 15, 22-24).
2. **Turn-to-object before text**: the visitor sees the thing, then reads about it (item 23).
3. **Numbered objects with thumbnails** in one row, matching numbered pins in the view (item 22).
4. **Panel order**: title, one media item, short note, credit (item 24). Their notes are 132-371 characters (25-65 words, one paragraph): a good length target.
5. **Voices as topics**: a face plus a question-like topic is a strong hook (item 18).
6. **Captions as one short line every ~3 s**, rendered in our own caption box from `cuechange` (item 12).
7. **Visible scene list plus Next/Previous**, linear spine with free jumps (item 3).
8. **Focus moved into panels and returned on close; scene-change live region; skip link; visible focus halo** (item 10).
9. **A non-spatial gallery of every object** (item 6).
10. **Calm visual language**: one typeface family, warm cream text on dark frosted glass over the full-bleed scene, round icon buttons, a single capsule of transport controls (UX §6). Our own palette and type come from the style kit; only the structure is copied.

### B2. Deliberate differences

## C. Architecture question: live three.js room with viewpoints vs baked 360 panoramas vs both

### What the teardown shows about Tapestry's camera
- Each stop stores a start and a zoom viewpoint; the camera eases between them during narration; each object has its own stored viewpoint and the camera flies there in 2 s (TECH §2.3). The flights were **not observed on screen** (pane hidden); parameters are from their code.
- Within one captured set they fly; between sets they cut to black with LOADING. Five of nine stops are flagged instant cut (UX §4). So the continuous "walk through the house" feeling exists only inside the downstairs and upstairs sets, and even there several stops cut.
- They ship the splat viewer because they have captures of the real house. We cannot capture the inductees' places (most no longer exist as they were), so a "live room" for us means a constructed three.js room, not a capture.

### Option costs (numbers from spikes S1/S2 unless stated)

Needs per-prop compression (Draco/KTX2) to get near the panorama size | Sum of the two only where live is used |

| Visual quality | Bake can afford soft shadows/AO offline; style choice from S2 | Real-time lighting limits quality on phones | Best of each |

| Archival layers | Separate untouched image layer in the panel (decided) | Same, or as textured planes in the room (risk of re-sampling the record) | Same |

### Answer
- **For the same visitor experience Tapestry gives per room** (narrate, look, click an object, see it framed, read a panel), the **baked panorama is simpler and smaller**: it reproduces every step except physical camera travel, which Tapestry itself drops on more than half of its stop changes. One baked page is about 0.9 MB versus megabytes of runtime models, and the accessibility layer is already working in S1.
- **Recommendation: both, split by place, from one source.** Scenes = baked panoramas (all 8-10 committed scenes). Entrance hall = the single live three.js room, where moving between portraits on stored viewpoints is the point (SYNTHESIS §1 item 3: tap-to-go nodes and a guided path). The bake already runs from the same `room.js` scene spec, so a scene can later be promoted to a live room without rewriting content.
- **Getting the Tapestry "flight" feel in a panorama**: animate yaw/pitch/zoom to the object (not just a cut), and fade between scenes. A fake dolly (zoom in while turning) gives most of the sense of approach. Under reduced motion all of this becomes jumps.
- Confidence: medium. Untested: live three.js room performance on iOS Safari and a Chromebook; PSV panorama memory on iOS (S1 §10). Both are on the vertical-slice device test list.

## D. Open questions for the user

1. **Gating**: show the object list and quote buttons from the start (our default, simpler, accessible) or hide them until the guide finishes, as Tapestry does?
2. **Separate "Dive Deeper" mode** or a permanent object list in every scene? (We recommend permanent.)
3. **Voices per scene**: Tapestry has three people per room. We have the guide plus the inductee's own quotes. Do you want a third voice type, e.g. short public-domain original recordings (where the inductee was recorded) or a curator-style "expert" written note? Any human expert video would need consent and is out of our current scope.
4. **Narration-timed camera drift**: keep a slow pan during each guide narration (Tapestry-like), or a still framed view (simpler, calmer)?
5. **Live room scope**: confirm the live three.js room is used only in the entrance hall, and scenes are baked panoramas. Or do you want one hero scene (for example the Einstein showcase) live as well?
6. **Panel on desktop**: Tapestry-style right side panel beside the scene (object stays visible), or a centred modal? We recommend the side panel on desktop and a bottom sheet on phones.
7. **3D objects**: which artifacts should be `<model-viewer>` objects (real scans or labeled reconstructions) versus photographs, given about 1-2 days per good reconstruction?
8. **Ambient sound and transition sounds**: include (off by default) or leave out entirely?
9. **End of each inductee**: next-inductee card, quiz and teacher kit; anything else (survey, IHH donation link) IHH would want?
10. **Overview stop**: should each inductee start with an overview stop (map/journey) the way Tapestry starts with an aerial fly-in, before the first room?
