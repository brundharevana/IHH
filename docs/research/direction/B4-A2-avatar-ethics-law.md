# B4-A2: Ethics and law of a realistic AI avatar vs a living painted portrait

Run 2026-10-09. Research only; not legal advice. Items marked COUNSEL need a qualified lawyer before launch. No commits. Free/open-source constraint noted but generator tooling for photoreal talking heads was NOT researched here (out of this route's scope).

Evidence tags: [V] read on the primary or near-primary page this session; [S] search summary or secondary commentary only; [K] my background knowledge, not re-verified this session; [J] my judgement; [U] not found or not tested.

Effort: about 27 searches and 28 page fetches. Blocked (HTTP 403 or bot wall): leginfo.legislature.ca.gov, law.justia.com, findlaw, si.edu. So the enacted text of Cal. Civ. ## 1. Bottom line (read this first)

1. Style decides the legal exposure more than the voice does. California's AB 1836 digital-replica rule, as analysed in the bill version I read, only reaches a simulation "that so closely resembles the individual's voice or likeness that a layperson would not be able to readily distinguish" it from the real thing [V, Senate Judiciary analysis, 2024-07-02 hearing]. A visibly painted portrait is outside that definition on its face; a photoreal talking head is inside it. COUNSEL on the enacted text.
2. For these five people AB 1836 probably does not bite even for a photoreal avatar, because of term and domicile (section 3) [J]. That is a legal-risk finding, not an ethical clearance.
3. The EU AI Act Article 50(4) is the live obligation for a photoreal avatar: a deployer must disclose a deep fake, defined as AI-generated or manipulated content that resembles existing persons and "would falsely appear to a person to be authentic or truthful" [V, artificialintelligenceact.eu, Art 3(60) and Art 50]. Disclosure is required from 2 August 2026 [S]. A painted portrait is, on the Commission's draft guidance, outside "realism" [S]. Whether the IHH audience is in the EU is unknown.
4. The ethics bar is higher than the legal bar. Public and professional reaction in 2025-2026 tracks "sourced and labelled" versus "invented and aestheticised" (section 6). A photoreal face speaking synthetic-voiced quotes sits closer to the criticised side than a painted face does, and Albright (died 2022) and Lamarr are the most sensitive cases.
5. Recommendation, with the reasoning in section 8: keep the living painted portrait as the default for all five, speak only verbatim cited words, label it on screen and in audio, and use a real recording instead of synthetic voice wherever one is clearly usable (Einstein 1945 VOA broadcast is the best lead; Albright federal-record audio second). Do not mix photoreal and painted across the cast. A photoreal option is defensible only for Muir and Naismith, and even then I would not choose it [J].

## 2. What exactly is being assessed

| Style | Description |
|---|---|
| A. Realistic synthetic | Photoreal or near-photoreal AI face, lip-synced, speaking an inductee's verbatim cited words in a synthetic, non-cloned voice |
| B. Living painted portrait | Our deterministic painterly filter on a verified public-domain photo (B3-S5), with subtle motion; same synthetic non-cloned voice, or a real recording |
| C. Real recording plus portrait | Genuine archival audio of the person (only where rights allow), shown over a painted or still portrait |

## 4. EU AI Act Article 50 and the 2026 labelling code

| Point | Finding |
|---|---|
| Definition | "deep fake" = AI-generated or manipulated image, audio or video that resembles existing persons, objects, places, entities or events and would falsely appear to a person to be authentic or truthful [V, Art 3(60)] |
| Duty | Deployer of a system that generates or manipulates a deep fake must disclose it [V, Art 50(4) as summarised]; information must be given "at the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure", clear and distinguishable, and meet accessibility requirements [V, Art 50(5)] |
| Artistic carve-down | Where content is part of an "evidently artistic, creative, satirical, fictional or analogous work", duty is limited to disclosing existence "in an appropriate manner that does not hamper the display or enjoyment of the work" [V]. The draft Commission guidance (8 May 2026) says this needs the character to be clearly recognisable from format, context and audience expectations and no predominant commercial purpose [S, srd-rechtsanwaelte.de]. A factual history exhibit is probably not "evidently fictional" [J] |
| Stylised content | Guidance says purely fictional, unrealistic depictions fall outside deep fake because they lack realism [S]. A visibly painted portrait of a real person is not addressed in what I read [U]; my reading is that it does not "falsely appear authentic" [J]. COUNSEL |
| Dates | Article 50 transparency applied from 2 Aug 2026; the Digital Omnibus gave only a narrow extra runway for machine-readable marking, to 2 Dec 2026 [S, whyaiman, didit, CSA note] |
| Code of Practice | Final Code on marking and labelling published 10 June 2026; voluntary; has deployer section on labelling deep fakes, an EU icon "or equivalent" labels, and audio disclaimers for audio-only content [S, Jones Day, SCL, via B2-R4] |
| Chatbot rule | Art 50(1): AI interaction must be disclosed unless obvious [V]. Relevant only if we add free-form chat, which the project rules out |
| Open | Whether pre-rendered synthetic narration over a painted portrait needs an audio disclaimer; whether IHH is a "deployer" within scope (personal non-professional use is outside the deployer definition [K], IHH is organisational); whether the audience includes EU users |

## 5. Real recordings: rights and candidates

Rule of thumb [K, plus V on term table]: US sound recordings made before 1972 now sit under federal protection (MMA 2018). Published recordings: before 1923 public domain from 1 Jan 2022; 1923-1946 protected for 95 years plus 5; 1947-1956 plus 15; others to 15 Feb 2067 [V, copyright.gov summary]. A user may file a notice of noncommercial use after a good-faith search, and the owner has 90 days to object [V, copyright.gov summary]. The spoken text can carry a separate copyright in the speech itself [K]. A recording made by US government staff is a federal work (17 U.S.C. 105) with no US copyright [K].

Pattern [J]: acceptable cases were licensed by a rights-holder or institution, scripted, and labelled (Dali Lives, Digital Einstein, pre-recorded survivor testimony). Criticised cases were unlabelled, free-form, or put invented words in a named person's mouth (SchoolAI, Founders Museum, fake Auschwitz portraits). Our design (verbatim cited words only, labelled) sits on the acceptable side for speech, but a photoreal face adds the "looks like evidence" risk the Auschwitz Memorial describes. A painted portrait is visibly an interpretation, which is the key ethical difference [J].

## 8. Recommendation for the best result

1. Default for all five: Style B, uniform across the cast, with the guide-narrator rule unchanged. Keep every inductee line to a verbatim, cited quote; put the citation on screen. [J]
2. Where cleared real audio exists, use it (Style C = B + real recording): Einstein 1945 first (after NARA check), Albright from confirmed federal records, Naismith 1939 only with KU/rights-holder permission.
3. Label in three places: visible badge ("Painted portrait made from a public-domain photograph"), voice line ("Voice: synthetic narrator, not [name]'s voice" or "Voice: archival recording, [source, date]"), and a methods page. This follows the Smithsonian formula (format + human role + AI role) and satisfies EU Art 50(5) timing ("first exposure") and accessibility (text alternative plus captions). [J]
4. Do not ship photoreal for Albright or Lamarr. If IHH insists on photoreal, restrict it to Muir and Naismith, label as a deep fake, and still expect the Auschwitz/AAM-style objection that a realistic face reads as evidence. I would not do it. [J]
5. Courtesy outreach list, no legal duty established: GreenLight / Hebrew University (Einstein), CMG (Lamarr), Albright family. Send a short notice with the label text before launch; keep replies as evidence. COUNSEL decides whether outreach is a precondition.
6. Write nothing that implies the inductee consented to or endorsed the exhibit.
